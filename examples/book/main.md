# The Reluctant Antenna
### *Or: Space Weather and Other Domestic Minor Inconveniences*

**Täpp Anders Sikvall (SM5UEI)**

---

## Introduction: On Order and Copper Wire

There is a widespread belief that modern technology is designed to make our
lives simpler. This theory is usually propagated by people who have never
attempted to suspend forty meters of enameled copper wire between two elderly
Swedish pine trees in sub-zero temperatures.

In Dalarna, winter arrives not as a weather event, but as a personality trait of
the landscape. The pine trees look stoic, the snow settles into a permanent
silence, and the air becomes crisp enough that you can hear a crow thinking three
parishes away. Under such conditions, a sensible person puts another log into the
tile stove, pours a cup of very dark coffee, and leaves nature to its own
devices.

A radio amateur, however, looks out at the trees and thinks: *That birch on the
ridge has an excellent radiation angle for fifteen megahertz.*

What follows is an entirely true account—give or take a few decibels of poetic
impedance matching—of an ordinary weekend dedicated to the noble art of coaxing
radio waves through the ionosphere, surviving unpredictable space weather, and
explaining to local wildlife why trees should not be used as grounding rods.
